# Desk cable management: advice principles and product layers (Amazon.co.jp)

Use this when the user asks how to tidy desk cables, or asks for products that implement a tidier setup.

## Advice principles (from user corrections)

- **Don't make "replace your cables" the method.** Cable length comes with each device and changes every time a device is swapped, so swapping in short cables isn't a general fix. Leave the cables alone and change two things: where each cable runs, and where its slack is stored.
- **Don't hide everything under the desk.** That makes every later change a crawl under the desk. Tier cables by how often they are touched:
  1. Set-and-forget (power, display, power strip, chargers, the host-to-hub uplink) goes under the desk in an open tray.
  2. Occasionally swapped peripherals plug into a hub within arm's reach: on the device's front, the desk side, or the front edge.
  3. Daily plug/unplug (USB stick, SD card, phone charger) stays on the desktop, on front ports or in a magnetic clip at the desk edge.
- **Keep the hidden layer adjustable.** Use an open mesh tray rather than a lidded box, velcro straps rather than zip ties, and open-slot clips rather than closed ones. Label both ends of each cable, and leave some slack behind each device so it can move forward.

## Product layers and search keywords

For each layer, read these fit constraints from the listing:

- **Hub next to the device.** Search for a purpose-built stand or hub for the user's exact device (`Mac Studio ハブ スタンド`): it fits under the device, puts the ports on the front, and needs one uplink cable. Check the port list, any "one bus-powered device at a time" limit, and shared total bandwidth. For generic clamp hubs (`USBハブ デスク クランプ`), check the board-thickness range, the uplink plug type (A or C), and whether there is a separate power input.
- **Under-desk tray** (`ケーブルトレー デスク 下 クランプ`). Check the board-thickness range, the required clamp depth (often ≥5 cm), the width or telescoping range, and the load rating. The clamp goes on the back edge, so a desk pushed flush against the wall must be moved forward first.
- **Power strip clamped to the desk edge.** Search `デスク タップ クランプ` for clamp-on strips, and `電源タップ 固定ホルダー クランプ` for holders that grip an existing strip or hub. Check the gripped width and board thickness, and which ways the cord can exit.
- **Quick-access clips** (`ケーブルホルダー マグネット`). The key spec is the maximum cable diameter: some models take only 3.5 mm phone cables, while multi-slot models take about 8 mm (HDMI, power cords). Note whether the adhesive is reusable.

End the answer with the measurements the user must take before ordering: board thickness, the gap between the desk and the wall, and the clamp depth available. For a desktop host with front ports (such as a Mac Studio), suggest moving wireless receivers and dongles to the front ports. That frees a hub port and removes a box from the desk.
